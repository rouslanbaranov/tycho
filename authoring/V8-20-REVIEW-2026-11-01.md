# v8-20 results review — 2026-11-01

Status: AI-authored review of the explicit 20-question subset. Not human review.

## Verdict

v8-20 is a useful methods pilot and a weak measurement. The v2 contract is
mechanically sound, but the judging layer is not reliable: raw agreement is
45%, Cohen's kappa is **-0.222**, and one judge passes 1/5 while another passes
5/5 on the same five answers. The headline **11/20** must be read as an
exploratory pilot, not a stable benchmark score.

## Verified counts

- answers: 20/20; judge files: 8/8; adjudication files: 2/2;
- consensus pass: 8/20;
- final pass: **11/20**;
- agreement: 9/20 (45%);
- kappa: **-0.222**;
- citation resolution: 20/20;
- support=2: 9/20;
- coverage=2: 19/20;
- mean words: 3,819.2; mean calls: 85.85;
- adjudicated: 11; adjudicator passes: 3.

95% Wilson intervals:

- final 11/20: 34.2%-74.2%;
- consensus 8/20: 21.9%-61.3%;
- book 7/12: 25.4%-74.6%;
- atlas 3/4: 30.1%-95.4%;
- modeling 1/4: 15.0%-85.0%.

## Finding 1 — judges are anti-correlated

Contingency table (both pass / A only / B only / neither):

```
8 | 4
7 | 1
```

Judge A passes 12/20; judge B passes 15/20. Kappa is negative because the
disagreements run in both directions and the marginals are close. Raw agreement
45% is not evidence of reliability; it is below chance correction.

Per-group pass counts:

- g0: 5/5 and 3/5;
- g1: 1/5 and 4/5;
- g2: 4/5 and 3/5;
- g3: 2/5 and 5/5.

Even with claim-level verdicts and the same rubric, judges disagree about what
counts as "supported" versus "partial".

## Finding 2 — claim-level rubric helped, but did not converge

The rubric caught concrete defects:

- Q326: core evidence used against its source meaning;
- Q305/Q309/Q311: partial support for missing branches or quoted specifics;
- Q339: missing saturation-run figures;
- Q310/Q338: missing quoted elements.

Adjudication overturned three failures (Q309, Q328, Q337) and confirmed eight.
This is better than a global task verdict, but the residual disagreement shows
that the rubric's boundary between partial and supported is still underspecified.

## Finding 3 — selection bias in the subset

The 20-question subset was not randomly drawn:

- 12 book answers were the first twelve already generated;
- atlas and M&S were the first four each;
- book is 60% of the subset (12/20), versus 50% in the full corpus.

The subset is therefore not representative of v8 and cannot support layer
comparisons. The layer intervals above overlap heavily.

## Finding 4 — mechanical success masks semantic failure

All 20 answers pass the mechanical contract:

- 20/20 citation resolution;
- 0 unresolved keys;
- 0 orphan evidence;
- coverage=2 for 19/20.

But only 9/20 have support=2 and 11/20 pass after adjudication. The contract
proves form, not correctness.

## Finding 5 — v8-20 is not v8

The full corpus has 50 questions. Only 20 were answered; 30 were not run. This
is an explicit pilot subset, not a 50-question benchmark and not comparable
with v6 or v7.

## What is supported

- the v2 contract can be enforced end-to-end;
- claim-level adjudication can identify missing quoted specifics;
- a 20-question pilot can be completed with dual judging;
- judge disagreement is measurable and large.

## What is not supported

- that 11/20 generalizes;
- that one layer is better than another;
- that the rubric is reliable (kappa negative);
- that v8 is complete;
- production readiness or human acceptance.

## Required next actions

1. Report v8-20 as a pilot; do not cite 11/20 without the interval and kappa.
2. Calibrate judges on known-good, partial and unsupported cases; target
   kappa >= 0.6 before a scored run.
3. Draw a random stratified subset, not the first generated answers.
4. Add a human or different-vendor sample review.
5. Freeze the rubric boundary for "partial" versus "supported".
6. Do not compare v8-20 with v6/v7.
7. v8-20 is consumed; a new run needs a new corpus or a frozen full v8.

## Limits

This review used saved results, judge files and adjudication outputs. It did
not re-read every source section and is not independent human review. No
book/atlas content changed.
