# Paired A/B: prompt v2 vs prompt v3 — 2026-11-01

Status: 20 paired dev questions, two independent AI pair judges. No human
review and no holdout claim.

## Design

- 20 dev questions: 10 book, 5 atlas, 5 modeling-and-simulation.
- Baseline: existing v2 answers from the earlier 100-agent run.
- Treatment: 20 fresh v3 agents with describe onboarding and evidence matrix.
- Answer order randomized per pair; judges saw answer_1/answer_2, not version.
- Two judges per five-pair group; consensus winner.

## Result

| Metric | v2 | v3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| consensus wins | 0 | **19** |
| ties | 1 | 1 |
| judge agreement | 19/20 pairs | — |
| mean CLI calls | 12.65 | **45.0** |
| total calls | 253 | 900 |
| mean answer words | 178 | **1,439** |
| mean citations | 3.9 | **12.55** |
| evidence rows | n/a | 13.9 mean |

v3 won 9/10 book, 5/5 atlas and 5/5 modeling-and-simulation pairs.

## Interpretation

The prompt change clearly changes answer shape: v3 is longer, cites more, and
covers more claims. The judges preferred it in 19/20 pairs.

The result is not a clean quality estimate:

- v3 is 8x longer, so coverage preference can be a length/comprehensiveness
  bias even though judges were told to ignore style;
- v2 answers came from an earlier run, not a same-session control;
- judges did not read evidence;
- no token accounting and no latency measurement;
- 20 dev questions, no holdout.

The correct conclusion is: v3 improves judged completeness at a large cost.
It does not establish a 95% quality win rate.

## Limits

- AI-only, no human review;
- no length-controlled comparison;
- no cost-adjusted metric;
- no fresh holdout;
- no book/atlas changes.
