## Claim and negative-evidence ledger

| Claim ID | Source + precise section | Admissible statement | Contraindication, metric scope or unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| N18.001 | "The problem" | On **m5.4xl 16-vCPU → m5.12xl 48-vCPU**, the expected ~3× throughput initially showed only **~25% uplift** at ~55% CPU target and **>50% worse mean latency** | Instance and Netflix GS2 workload specific, not JVM-general |
| N18.002 | "False Sharing", "True Sharing" | ~12% fast nodes correlated with cache-line placement; padding removed false sharing, but residual true sharing limited throughput to ~150 RPS; disabling secondary-superclass cache writes behind a flag raised it to **~350 RPS**, company-reported **3.5×** relative to initial larger-instance baseline | Exact 2022 JDK implementation and CPU, no independent experiment; not advice to change present JDK internals |
| U03.001 | "Motivation", "Challenges" | Uber's test showed **>30% disk-space savings** for MyRocks versus InnoDB after its compression layer yielded only ~5% additional gains; MySQL 8.0 was a prerequisite | Same MySQL wire/client API, but LSM read/compaction trade-offs and upgrade costs remain |
| U03.002 | "MySQL 8.0 Upgrade", "MyRocks Migration", "Lessons" | The migration operated partition/follower/leader and region stages with **table-count/checksum**, query replay/plan inspection, Raft replica safety, region comparison and rollback; full Schemaless and *some* Docstore migrated | Full migration took years; 8.0 upgrade took over a year, MyRocks increased CPU/IO for some use cases; not an instantaneous no-risk engine toggle |
| U04.001 | "Background", "Post-release observations", "Key metrics" | Uber reported **4,500→5,000 services**, **7,000→50,000 deployments/week** and **7%→65% of services automatically deployed** between 2022 and March 2024 | Publisher-correlated before/after, not independently randomized intervention |
| U04.002 | "No increase in production incidents", "New challenges" | Reported >50% fewer incidents per 1,000 code changes during the period, **explicitly says causality cannot be claimed**; system responded to shared-library rapid rollout risk with service-tier staged deployment | Avoid 'CD alone caused 50% improvement' or eliminating all rollout regressions |
| U05.001 | "Shadowing", "Validation" | For payment-auth migration, company replayed **cached external PSP responses**, because live double-calling payment providers would cause side effects, and checked both request arguments and new AuthHold state | A source-specific shadow-safety mechanism, not generic duplication of all production effects |
| U05.002 | "Validation", "Roll out" | Dozens of mismatches were found in days of trials; Uber deployed one payment method at a time, then employee cohorts then **0.25%, 1%, 5% → 100%** | Historic staged payment-service migration, not a guaranteed or universal cohort schedule |
| U06.001 | "Quota-Based Rate Limiting", "Identifying the Right Signal" | Stateless query-layer Redis quotas were too imprecise, added a hop and failure point; moved overload protection toward storage nodes where concurrency/queuing was visible | QPS or byte accounting alone can underweight expensive scans or hot keys |
| U06.002 | "Controlled Delay", "Cinnamon Replaces CoDel", "Scorecard" | Independent read/write/slow queues and CoDel protected base availability but treated priorities equally; later Cinnamon offered priority-aware shedding; per-tenant scorecards and node-local regulators handled skew | CoDel's static thresholds caused retry thundering herd and high-priority drops; do not claim first design was universally successful |
| SH03.001 | "We are building more modular code" | Shopify combined modular Rails monolith with **extracted storefront rendering**, reporting **4× faster cache fills** and **5× faster page rendering** after extraction and refactoring | One storefront deployment and source-reported benchmark; **falsifies 'Shopify never extracted services'** |
| ME01.001 | "Objects and associations", "TAO data model and API" | TAO stores typed graph objects/associations and offers point/range/count queries rather than general graph traversals and server-side intersections | Workload-shaped social graph serving API, not a general transactional graph database |
| ME01.002 | "Implementation", "Consistency" | Facebook used follower/leader caching over sharded MySQL, with **eventual consistency default** and stronger consistency optional at higher cost | Not a default for payment authorization or strict ledger correctness |
| ME02.001 | First-party NSDI'13 announcement | Facebook describes hot-key fanout, fast-changing read-heavy graph and memcache caching billions of requests/second in 2013; details belong in cited NSDI paper | Announcement is **not** a comprehensive algorithmic source; don't infer specific invalidation protocol from brief prose |
| ME03.001 | "The Migration Lifecycle" / "Shadow Phase" / "Reverse Shadow Phase" | Meta migrated a CDC ingestion platform by comparing **row counts and checksums**, shadowing the same source to separate tables then reversing old/new output roles while keeping both active | Count+checksum is a stronger defined check than count alone, but depends on same snapshot/partition and canonicalization |
| ME03.002 | "Handling Rollout and Rollback" | Meta used bad-quality partition metadata and backfill to stop CDC error propagation; company reports **100% of jobs migrated and old service deprecated** in May 2026 | Publisher's success claim; not an independently audited exact data identity proof |
